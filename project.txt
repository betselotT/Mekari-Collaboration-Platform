Abstract 
In the dynamic realm of technical education and professional growth, students and practitioners in 
engineering fields face substantial hurdles in securing prompt, effective peer support. Platforms like 
Stack Overflow offer asynchronous Q&A, Microsoft Teams enables team communication, Discord 
supports casual voice chats, and AI tools such as ChatGPT provide instant aid yet they remain 
fragmented, delayed, and deficient in unified real-time collaboration. This leads to extended issue 
resolution, isolated knowledge sharing, and reduced mentorship, especially in settings like Addis 
Ababa Science and Technology University (AASTU). 
This capstone project counters these issues through MEKARI, a lightweight full-stack web platform 
designed for interdisciplinary student technical collaboration. Core aims encompass real-time voice 
and whiteboard integration via Google Meet APIs, a context-aware AI chatbot for tailored 
troubleshooting, subject-based expert matching, and gamification via points, badges, and leaderboards 
to boost engagement. Employing agile Scrum with bi-weekly sprints, the architecture utilizes 
Node.js/Express backend, MongoDB persistence, Redis for messaging, and Next.js/Tailwind CSS 
frontend for device-agnostic access. Requirements were elicited from 20–30 stakeholders via surveys 
and interviews, modeled with UML for dynamics, and prototyped iteratively. Functional elements 
include threaded chats and issue logging; non-functional targets cover ≤2-second latency and WCAG 
2.1 AA accessibility. Modular implementation validated features like weighted matching and AI 
escalation using Cypress/Mocha tests and UAT. 
Results validate efficacy: 91% matching accuracy, 88% AI precision in 3.7 seconds, SUS score of 
85.8—exceeding peers. Feedback praises live-session fluidity and gamification's motivational impact. 
MEKARI's import lies in transforming student collaboration: it curtails delays, builds searchable 
knowledge repositories, and nurtures mentorship, aligning with AASTU's equitable education goals. 
As an open-source prototype, it inspires institutional scaling, AI advancements, and bridges gaps in 
real-time peer support, enriching software engineering education and practice.

Chapter One 
Introduction 
In the contemporary landscape of science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) 
education, collaborative learning stands as a cornerstone for innovation and skill development. 
As global economies increasingly rely on technical expertise to drive progress, fostering 
effective peer support mechanisms among students has become imperative.  
The current state of affairs in technical peer support reveals stark disparities. Students endure 
heightened academic pressures, with studies indicating that they exhibit a significantly lower 
propensity to seek help compared to peers in other disciplines often due to stigma, time 
constraints, and perceived barriers such as lack of resource awareness. Globally, unresolved 
technical issues exacerbate these challenges; professionals and students alike lose nearly three 
hours per week navigating technology glitches, translating to substantial productivity deficits. 
In STEM fields, this inefficiency contributes to broader economic tolls: projections estimate 
3.5 million unfilled STEM jobs by the end of 2025, alongside annual losses of up to $480 
billion in the United States alone from talent shortages. In higher education, while adoption of 
online collaboration platforms has surged eLearning uptake increased by 300% between 2019 
and 2021, and 86% of institutions now integrate AI tools the hybrid model's permanence is 
embraced by nearly 70% of administrators, underscoring a shift toward digital augmentation 
of traditional learning. 
Despite these advancements, existing systems fall short in delivering seamless, real-time 
support tailored to technical disciplines. Platforms like Stack Overflow provide asynchronous 
Q&A but impose delays averaging hours to days, while tools such as Microsoft Teams and 
Discord facilitate communication yet lack automated expert matching or integrated visual aids 
like whiteboards. Standalone AI solutions, including ChatGPT, offer instant responses but 
isolate users from community validation and mentorship. Attempted remedies, such as 
institutional peer-mentoring programs, have shown promise reducing therapy sessions by over 
two per participant in some digital peer support initiatives but remain siloed, underutilized, and 
insufficiently scalable across diverse student cohorts. These gaps not only prolong problem 
resolution but also hinder the cultivation of a collaborative ecosystem essential for student 
success. 
1 
This project introduces MEKARI as a student-led response, a lightweight platform designed 
to bridge these voids through agile, inclusive innovation, empowering AASTU's engineering 
community and beyond. 
1.1. Statement of the Problem 
Individuals in diverse technical fields frequently face challenges in obtaining timely, precise, 
and effective support for complex problems. Current solutions ranging from comprehensive 
software for remote collaboration to online forums and social media communities are often 
inefficient, fragmented, or difficult to navigate, resulting in delayed problem resolution and 
limited opportunities for knowledge exchange and mentorship. 
There is a clear need for a streamlined, accessible platform that connects users with relevant 
technical expertise in real time. Such a platform should enable interactive voice and whiteboard 
collaboration, incorporate AI-driven guidance for context-sensitive assistance, and foster 
community engagement through gamification and rewards. Additionally, robust issue tracking 
would create an evolving knowledge repository to inform future interactions. By integrating 
these features, the platform aims to enhance the efficiency and quality of technical support, 
facilitate rapid problem-solving, and promote collaborative learning and professional growth 
across technical disciplines. 
1.2. Objectives 
1.2.1. General Objective 
To develop an AI-assisted collaboration platform that connects users seeking technical support 
with experienced mentors and peers, emphasizing real-time interactive collaboration, context
aware AI guidance, and community-driven knowledge sharing across diverse technical disciplines. 
1.2.2. Specific Objectives 
• Study and analyze existing technical collaboration and support platforms to identify their 
strengths, weaknesses, limitations, and best practices in areas such as real-time 
communication, expert matching, and user engagement. 
2 
● Elicit and prioritize user requirements through Google Form surveys and interviews 
targeting students, professionals, and experts from multiple technical fields to ensure the 
platform addresses real pain points in timely and effective support. 
● Design and implement subject tagging and intelligent expert-matching mechanisms to 
automatically categorize queries and connect users with relevant available mentors or 
peers. 
● Develop a responsive chat interface supporting threaded conversations, code/image 
attachments, notifications, mentions, and seamless transitions to live sessions. 
● Integrate real-time voice communication and interactive whiteboard features to enable 
collaborative problem-solving and visual explanations during live sessions. 
● Incorporate a context-aware AI chatbot that provides personalized troubleshooting 
guidance, suggestions, and platform navigation support based on conversation context and 
logged issues. 
● Create customizable user profiles allowing individuals to highlight expertise areas, set 
availability status, and showcase professional/academic backgrounds for efficient 
matching. 
● Design and implement a gamification and reward system including points, badges, 
leaderboards, and recognition features to motivate active participation, timely responses, 
and quality contributions. 
● Build a repository for logging technical issues, solutions, and interactions to enable 
search functionality and data-driven recommendations for future queries. 
● Ensure responsive and accessible design optimized for multiple devices (desktops, 
tablets, smartphones) following best practices for usability, navigation, and inclusivity. 
● Implement moderation tools, security controls (e.g., authentication, spam prevention), 
and administrative features to maintain a safe and respectful community environment. 
● Develop usage analytics to track trending topics, active contributors and engagement 
metrics for continuous platform improvement and community insights. 
1.3. Scope and Limitation 
Scope: 
This project focuses on developing Mekari, a lightweight peer-support platform for users 
across diverse technical fields. The platform facilitates real-time collaboration, knowledge 
3 
sharing, and problem-solving through features such as: 
• Subject-specific channels: Focused group discussions and one-on-one assistance for 
technical topics. 
• Real-time helper availability: Quickly identify and connect with available experts. 
• Threaded conversations and notifications: Organized communication with mentions 
and alerts. 
• Gamification and reputation systems: Badges, rankings, and recognition to encourage 
active participation. 
• Live session integration: Transition from chat to voice or video sessions via platforms 
like Google Meet. 
• AI chatbot assistance: Context-aware guidance, study recommendations, and platform 
navigation support. 
• Voice and whiteboard collaboration: Real-time visual problem-solving for enhanced 
comprehension. 
• Technical issue logging and solution repository: Build an evolving knowledge base to 
improve support efficiency. 
Limitations: 
The initial development phase will exclude resource-intensive tools such as code editors, CAD 
or 3D modeling software, circuit simulators, process simulation modules, automated grading 
systems, and monetization features like subscriptions or paid coaching. 
By focusing on core communication, expert matching, and interactive support, Mekari aims to 
provide an accessible, efficient, and collaborative environment for technical problem-solving 
while remaining lightweight and compatible across devices and network conditions.

1.4. Methodology 
This project adopts a user-centered, agile development approach to create a lightweight 
platform that supports real-time technical collaboration and peer mentorship. 
Research Design and Data Collection: 
Requirements will be gathered through questionnaires and semi-structured interviews from 
users across multiple technical disciplines. This ensures inclusion of key features such as 
expert matching, AI-assisted guidance, and voice/whiteboard collaboration. 
Development Approach: 
The Scrum framework within agile methodology will guide development through biweekly 
sprints, allowing iterative feature implementation, stakeholder engagement, and rapid 
refinement based on feedback. 
System Design and Implementation: 
• Backend: Node.js with Express for RESTful APIs; MongoDB for user profiles, chat 
data, and technical issue logs. 
• Frontend: Next.js with Tailwind CSS for a responsive, accessible interface across 
desktops, tablets, and smartphones. 
• Real-time Collaboration: Google Meet APIs to support live voice communication and 
whiteboard interactions. 
• AI Integration: A context-aware chatbot leveraging NLP frameworks for personalized 
guidance and study recommendations. 
Testing and Evaluation: 
• Unit Testing: Cypress for frontend components and Mocha for backend APIs. 
• Integration Testing: Postman to validate interoperability of chat, notifications, and live 
sessions. 
• User Acceptance Testing (UAT): Pilot testing with 20–30 diverse users; feedback 
collected via surveys and interviews. 
5 
• Performance Metrics: Quantitative measures include peer-matching accuracy, 
collaboration latency, and chatbot relevance. 
• Qualitative Analysis: Thematic analysis of user feedback to identify usability 
improvements and enhance platform effectiveness. 
This methodology ensures the platform is robust, user-friendly and responsive, fostering 
efficient technical problem-solving and collaborative learning.

Individuals in technical fields often face complex challenges where timely peer support is

simplicity.

• Accelerated problem resolution: Intelligent matching connects users with peers

Chapter Two 
Literature Review 
Over the last 20 years, technical support and knowledge sharing have transformed 
dramatically. What began with static mailing lists and forums has evolved into lively, real-time 
spaces powered by AI and collaborative tools. This literature review serves two main purposes: 
it traces the evolution of these platforms, highlighting how each innovation tackled (or 
sometimes created) new challenges, and it builds a framework showing why Mekari is a smart 
blend of proven strengths that fixes ongoing weaknesses. 
A close look at existing studies uncovers a clear pattern: platforms excel in one area but often 
fall short in others. Stack Overflow nailed structured knowledge storage but skipped real-time 
teamwork. Tools like Teams and Discord delivered instant chats at the cost of lasting records. 
AI chatbots offer quick replies, yet they miss deeper context and human touch. This review 
positions our work amid these partial fixes, explaining how Mekari bridges that tricky balance 
between machine speed and human insight, immediacy and endurance. 
What stands out isn't a checklist of features to copy, but real insights into how technical 
communities thrive: how trust builds, expertise gets spotted, people step up to help strangers, 
and platforms stay vibrant long-term. 
2.1. Study Related Works 
2.1.1 Stack Overflow 
Stack Overflow, launched in 2008, changed the game for technical Q&A with strict formatting, 
tags, voting, and a sharp line between questions and chit-chat. Treude et al. (2011) showed how 
this setup sparked accidental expertise, where developers uncover their own knowledge by spelling 
out solutions. 
Less discussed is the emotional side: Stack Overflow assumes you're competent. Questions must 
show research and effort, which respects experts but intimidates newcomers. Technically, its big 
flaw is the time gap. Answers live forever via search but offer no live help. Vasilescu et al. (2014) 
noted median response times around 11 minutes for answered questions, but many (especially 
niche ones) go unanswered. 
10 
2.1.2 Discord  
Discord’s evolution from a gaming-focused platform into a widely used space for technical 
communities, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic, provides an important case study in 
modern collaboration tools. Beyond features such as voice channels, threads, and screen sharing, 
Discord introduced strong presence awareness: users can see who is online, which encourages 
spontaneous interaction and informal knowledge exchange. 
However, this informality also introduces limitations. Conversations move quickly, valuable 
technical insights are often buried in long message histories, and search functionality is poorly 
suited for retrieving detailed or previously discussed solutions. As a result, the same questions 
frequently reappear, indicating weak knowledge retention. 
Microsoft Teams, by contrast, is designed for workplace collaboration and integrates closely with 
productivity tools such as Microsoft Office. Its structured channels and document integration aim 
to improve organization. Nevertheless, Wang et al. (2020) identify a phenomenon they describe as 
collaboration overload, characterized by excessive notifications, fragmented conversations, and 
frequent context switching, all of which can reduce focus and hinder deep problem-solving. 
2.1.3 AI Chatbots 
AI-assisted tools such as GitHub Copilot and ChatGPT represent a third wave of technical support 
systems. These tools combine code generation, contextual awareness, and conversational 
interfaces to assist developers during problem-solving. Ziegler et al. (2022) report that developers 
using GitHub Copilot completed tasks up to 55% faster. However, the authors also caution against 
an illusion of understanding, where confident and fluent AI-generated output may conceal subtle 
errors, requiring users to develop stronger verification and critical evaluation skills. 
AI chatbots transform traditional question–answer interactions into iterative dialogues, enabling 
rapid exploration of solutions. Despite these advantages, Xu et al. (2023) find that such systems 
perform poorly on ill-defined problems, edge cases, and domain-specific reasoning that is 
underrepresented in their training data. As a result, effective use often involves what experienced 
users describe as collaborative skepticism: leveraging AI for rapid prototyping while relying on 
human judgment to assess correctness, context, and underlying rationale. 
11 
2.1.4 Gamification 
Gamification boosts activity, but points alone can kill intrinsic drive. Zhang (2018) tracked Stack 
Overflow users and saw reputation chasers burn out without building true community ties. Toda 
et al. (2019) pushed for tailored, multi-faceted rewards that honor mentors, niche solvers, and 
builders not just answer volume. 
In summary, while Stack Overflow preserves knowledge, Teams/Slack organize teams, Discord 
enables casual voice hangouts, and AI chatbots deliver instant answers, none provide a unified 
lightweight platform that combines real-time expert availability indicators, automated subject
based matching, integrated voice-plus-whiteboard collaboration, gamified rapid-response 
incentives, and a searchable technical-issue repository under one roof. These fragmented solutions 
force users to juggle multiple tools, endure delays, or settle for incomplete help. Mekari directly 
addresses these gaps by synthesizing the strongest elements persistent threads, live sessions, AI 
augmentation, and reputation-driven motivation into a single, accessible, discipline-agnostic 
environment optimized for immediate, visual, and rewarding technical peer support.

● Structuring (2008-2012): Stack Overflow proved structure (voting, tags, reps) beats chaotic

● Real-Time (2016-2020): Discord showed instant interaction is key for in-the-moment needs like 
deadlines or flow states. 
● Multimedia (2019-present): Pandemic tools added voice, video, whiteboards—text alone falls

● AI Assistance (2021-present): LLMs handle routine queries instantly, freeing humans for the

• Engineering foundation: Andrade et al. (2020) provide a concrete model of an

Chapter Three 
Problem Analysis and Modeling 
This chapter outlines the core challenges in existing technical peer support systems and 
establishes MEKARI's foundational specifications as a capstone project by software 
engineering students at Addis Ababa Science and Technology University (AASTU). Its 
purpose is to dissect issues like fragmentation and latency in tools such as Stack Overflow and 
Discord, while defining functional and non-functional requirements through stakeholder input. 
UML models, including use cases, sequence diagrams, and entity relationship diagrams, 
provide a validated framework for the solution. 
This phase is crucial because it converts identified problems into precise, actionable designs, 
reducing implementation risks and promoting student collaboration. It links literature gaps 
from Chapter Two to forward design in Chapter Four, enabling an efficient platform that boosts 
interdisciplinary knowledge sharing among AASTU's engineering students. 
3.1. Existing System and Its Problems 
The current landscape of technical peer support is severely fragmented, compelling users across 
academic and professional settings to rely on an assortment of disconnected tools that collectively 
fail to deliver timely, interactive, and effective assistance. Students, developers, engineers, 
researchers, and technical professionals routinely switch between static Q&A forums, real-time 
messaging applications, standalone AI assistants, and institutional collaboration platforms. While 
each tool excels in isolated aspects, none provide a unified environment capable of supporting the 
full lifecycle of technical problem-solving from instant guidance to live multimodal collaboration 
and long-term knowledge retention. 
The core problems of the existing ecosystem are:  
1. Severe Tool Fragmentation: No single platform integrates persistent knowledge storage, 
real-time multimodal collaboration, intelligent expert matching, AI augmentation, and 
motivational incentives. 
2. Inefficient Expert Discovery and Availability Awareness: Identifying who is both 
knowledgeable and currently free to help remains manual, slow, and unreliable. 
16 
3. Absence of Seamless Voice + Whiteboard Collaboration: Critical for visual and spatial 
technical topics, yet consistently requires launching external applications. 
4. Lack of Incentives for Rapid, High-Quality Voluntary Help” Volunteers receive little 
recognition or reward for timely, effective assistance. 
5. Poor Reuse of Solved Problems: Solutions are scattered across forum threads, private chats, 
or ephemeral AI sessions, preventing community-wide learning. 
These systemic deficiencies result in delayed problem resolution, diminished learning outcomes, 
and unnecessary frustration across technical disciplines. Mekari directly confronts this fragmented 
status quo by delivering a lightweight, unified web platform that combines real-time expert 
availability indicators, seamless voice-and-whiteboard sessions, context-aware AI assistance, 
gamified rapid-response incentives, and a structured, searchable repository of resolved technical 
issues all within an inclusive, discipline-agnostic environment designed to accelerate 
understanding and foster sustainable collaborative growth. 
Traditional question-and-answer platforms, led by Stack Overflow, remain the gold standard for 
archiving high-quality, searchable solutions backed by community voting and reputation systems. 
However, their strictly asynchronous nature means responses often take hours or days, real-time 
clarification is impossible, and there is no support for voice communication or interactive 
whiteboards features essential for explaining complex algorithms, system designs, mathematical 
proofs, or hardware schematics. The platform’s rigid formatting requirements and downvote
heavy culture further deter newcomers and discourage exploratory or beginner-level questions. 
Enterprise and academic collaboration tools such as Microsoft Teams, Slack, and Google 
Workspace offer persistent channels, threaded conversations, file sharing, and integrated video 
conferencing. These are effective within known teams but are poorly suited for open, cross
organizational expert discovery. Users must manually search directories or rely on personal 
networks to locate domain experts, and there are no mechanisms to indicate real-time availability, 
expertise depth, or incentives for voluntary assistance from external peers. 
17 
Community-oriented real-time platforms like Discord have gained massive adoption among 
students and open-source contributors due to always-on voice channels, screen sharing, and a low
friction interface. Many learning communities maintain informal “help desks” where members 
drop into voice channels to assist others. Despite this organic success, Discord lacks native 
whiteboard tools, structured expert-matching algorithms, centralized solution logging, and 
portable reputation systems. Knowledge remains trapped within individual servers, cross-server 
discoverability is virtually non-existent, and moderation quality varies dramatically. 
Modern standalone AI assistants (e.g., ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, Claude, Phind) have 
transformed access to instant technical explanations and code generation. Their 24/7 availability 
and natural-language understanding make them excellent first-line resources. However, they 
operate in isolation: answers can hallucinate without peer review, there is no escalation path to 
human experts when explanations require interactive diagramming or verbal nuance, and they do 
not foster community validation, mentorship, or collective knowledge accumulation. 
Specialized institutional mentoring systems and tutoring platforms typically focus on 
asynchronous scheduling, progress tracking, and administrative oversight. They rarely support ad
hoc real-time collaboration and are almost always confined within organizational boundaries, 
preventing broader peer-to-peer knowledge exchange. 
In practice, users are forced to juggle four to six separate tools simultaneously, as illustrated in

3.2. Specifying the Requirements of the Proposed Solution 
The process of specifying requirements for the proposed MEKARI platform involves systematic 
elicitation and documentation of stakeholder needs to ensure the system effectively addresses the 
shortcomings of existing technical support solutions. Requirements gathering is conducted through 
a combination of structured Google Forms surveys distributed to potential users across various 
technical disciplines, including software engineering, electrical engineering, and mechanical 
engineering students and professionals, as well as direct observations of user interactions with 
current platforms such as Stack Overflow, Discord servers, and remote collaboration tools. The 
surveys collect quantitative and qualitative data on pain points, desired features, and usage 
preferences, while observations provide insights into real-world challenges like navigation 
difficulties and delayed responses. Responses from over 20 participants are analysed thematically 
to prioritize features that enhance real-time collaboration and knowledge sharing. This elicitation 
process directly informs the definition of requirements, ensuring the proposed solution overcomes 
issues of fragmentation, inefficiency, and limited interactivity identified in the existing systems. 
The functional requirements outline the core capabilities the system provides to users. These 
include User Authentication and Registration, User Profile Management, Availability Status 
19 
Management, Expertise Tagging, Question Posting with Subject Tagging, Expert Matching and 
Notification, Real-Time Chat Messaging, Attachment Support (Code and Images), Live Voice and 
Whiteboard Session Integration, AI Chatbot Assistance, Context-Aware Response Generation, 
Escalation to Human Experts, Thread Resolution Marking, Issue Logging and Repository Search, 
Solution Recommendation, Gamification Points Award, Badge and Ranking System, Leaderboard 
Display, Notification Preferences, Push and Email Alerts, Content Reporting, Moderation Tools, 
Spam Prevention, Admin Analytics Dashboard, Trending Topics Analysis, and Cross-Device 
Synchronization. 
The non-functional requirements define the quality attributes and constraints that shape the 
system's performance, usability, and reliability. These encompass Performance and Latency, Real
Time Responsiveness, Scalability, Concurrent User Support, Security and Encryption, 
Authentication Strength, Privacy Compliance, Reliability and Uptime, Fault Tolerance, Usability 
and Interface Design, Accessibility Standards, Responsiveness Across Devices, Onboarding Ease, 
Maintainability and Code Quality, Test Coverage, Data Integrity, Search Accuracy, Rate Limiting, 
and Offline Support Capabilities. 
These requirements collectively guide the design and implementation of MEKARI, ensuring a 
focused, user-centered platform that delivers efficient, secure, and engaging technical 
collaboration.

Chapter Four 
System Design 
4.1. Overview 
This chapter presents the system design of MEKARI, a lightweight and full-stack collaboration 
platform developed to support real-time technical support, peer-to-peer learning, and 
knowledge sharing across different technological fields. The system design serves as the bridge 
between the requirements identified in the previous chapter and the technical implementation 
that will bring the platform to life. It focuses on defining the structure, components, 
technologies, and integration methods required to achieve the platform’s core purpose. 
MEKARI is built around features such as interactive voice support, real-time whiteboard 
collaboration, AI-driven guidance, expert-user matching, gamification, and community 
moderation. Since the goal is to develop a lightweight platform, the design intentionally avoids 
building heavy infrastructures that would complicate development and increase operational 
costs. Instead, the platform integrates reliable third-party services for components such as live 
communications and AI processing, allowing developers to focus on the features that make 
MEKARI unique. 
The design emphasizes modularity, scalability, and user-friendly operation, ensuring that the 
platform remains responsive across multiple device types and capable of handling real-time 
interactions with minimal delay. It also incorporates modern web technologies and established 
design practices that enable maintainability, extensibility, and alignment with the project’s 
constraints. In essence, this chapter transforms user needs into a practical architectural 
blueprint that guides development and reduces implementation risks. 
4.2. Specifying the Design Goals 
The design goals of MEKARI arise directly from the general and specific objectives in Section 
1.2 as well as the functional and non-functional requirements described in Chapter Three. 
These goals serve as principles that shape the platform’s architecture and ensure that the system 
meets expectations in terms of performance, usability, reliability, and sustainability. 
46 
A major design goal is performance and real-time responsiveness. Since MEKARI offers voice 
interaction, collaborative whiteboarding, and messaging, it must maintain very low latency so 
that users can communicate smoothly without noticeable delays. The platform therefore adopts 
event-driven technologies such as WebSockets and WebRTC to ensure that updates, messages, 
and interactions appear almost instantly on all connected devices. This level of responsiveness 
is essential for creating a natural and effective collaborative environment. 
Scalability and maintainability also form central goals. As MEKARI grows, it must support an 
increasing number of users without system slowdowns. The architecture is therefore organized 
into modular components that can be developed, deployed, and scaled independently. This 
approach makes it easier to update or replace certain features without affecting the entire 
system, which reduces technical debt and enables long-term sustainability. 
Security and privacy are equally important. Because users will share information, collaborate 
in real-time, and participate in community spaces, the platform must implement strong 
authentication and authorization mechanisms, encrypt all communication, and prevent 
unauthorized access or misuse. User privacy is protected through compliance with data 
protection standards such as GDPR, while community safety is strengthened through 
moderation features that identify and minimize abusive behavior. 
The design also emphasizes usability and accessibility. MEKARI aims to be intuitive enough 
for beginners while offering advanced tools for experts. Its interface is optimized for desktops, 
tablets, and mobile devices, ensuring a consistent experience regardless of screen size. By 
adhering to accessibility guidelines, the platform becomes more inclusive for users with 
different needs and abilities. 
Interoperability and extensibility guide the system’s integration strategy. MEKARI is not built 
as a closed system; instead, it integrates external services such as Google Meet or AI APIs to 
expand its capabilities. This loose coupling makes it possible to add or replace services later 
without major architectural changes. 
47 
Reliability and fault tolerance ensure that MEKARI remains available even when certain 
components fail. The design incorporates mechanisms such as graceful degradation, 
continuous monitoring, data caching, and fallback options so that users can continue working 
even under imperfect conditions. This is especially important for real-time systems where 
interruptions would significantly affect the user experience. 
Finally, the system is intentionally designed to be lightweight and cost-effective. Rather than 
building expensive custom features such as proprietary voice engines or simulation systems, 
MEKARI leverages existing open-source technologies and cloud services. This keeps costs 
manageable and ensures that the platform remains within the project’s scope while delivering 
high-quality functionality. 
4.3. System Design 
The system design of MEKARI brings together the architectural structure, subsystem 
decomposition, data organization, integration methods, and security strategies needed to 
implement the platform. The architecture follows a client–server model supported by event
driven communication for real-time features. In this model, the client interface interacts with 
the backend through both REST APIs and WebSocket channels. REST handles standard 
operations such as authentication, profile management, and storing content, while WebSockets 
enable instant updates for chat, whiteboard collaboration, notifications, and presence status. 
The addition of third-party services bridges gaps in functionality without adding unnecessary 
system weight. 
The system is divided into several subsystems, each responsible for a core area of functionality. 
The user management subsystem handles authentication, authorization, user profiles, and role 
assignments such as experts and administrators. The collaboration subsystem manages chat 
messages, whiteboard interactions, forum discussions, and other real-time communication 
tools. The voice and live session subsystem connects with services such as Google Meet or 
WebRTC libraries to allow users to join or host live discussions. The AI guidance subsystem 
communicates with external AI APIs to provide automated suggestions and assistance based 
on user queries or context. Expert matching is handled through a subsystem that analyzes user 
needs, topics, and availability to recommend the most suitable expert. Gamification is 
implemented through a subsystem that tracks user activity, assigns points and badges, and 
48 
maintains leaderboards to encourage active participation. Meanwhile, the community 
moderation subsystem ensures the platform remains safe by handling reports, detecting spam, 
and enabling administrators to review suspicious activities. Finally, the data and storage 
subsystem organizes and maintains all platform data, ensuring efficient retrieval and storage. 
MEKARI uses a flexible NoSQL database that supports semi-structured and unstructured data, 
which is ideal for storing user profiles, chat logs, whiteboard events, reports, achievements, 
and other dynamic content. This structure allows rapid read and write operations, a crucial 
requirement for real-time systems. Collections such as users, sessions, messages, posts, and 
reports are designed to efficiently handle growing volumes of activity without rigid schema 
limitations. 
Integration plays a major role in MEKARI’s architecture. Instead of reinventing complex 
systems, MEKARI incorporates third-party services for tasks such as voice calls, AI 
processing, and large-file storage. These integrations are implemented using well-defined 
interfaces so that services can be replaced or upgraded without affecting other parts of the 
system. This approach keeps MEKARI lightweight and adaptable for future expansions. 
Security is embedded throughout the system. MEKARI uses encrypted communication, secure 
identity management, input validation, and role-based access control to ensure that only 
authorized users can perform specific actions. Anti-abuse measures such as rate limiting and 
spam detection are included to protect real-time communication channels. The system also 
maintains logs and monitoring mechanisms to quickly detect abnormal behavior or system 
errors. 
By aligning the system design with the functional and non-functional requirements defined in 
earlier chapters, MEKARI’s architecture ensures complete coverage of user needs. Every 
major requirement whether related to collaboration tools, expert matching, AI guidance, 
gamification, or moderation is accounted for in the subsystem design. Likewise, non-functional 
requirements such as responsiveness, accessibility, scalability, security, and cost effectiveness 
are addressed through architectural decisions, technological choices, and integration strategies.

4.3.1. Proposed Software Architecture 
The proposed software architecture for MEKARI adopts a layered, full-stack approach that 
separates concerns across presentation, application, and data layers, facilitating maintainability 
and extensibility. At its core is a client-server model enhanced with real-time WebSocket 
connections for collaborative features, ensuring seamless interactions without page reloads. 
• Presentation Layer (Frontend): Built with Next.js (React-based framework) and styled 
using Tailwind CSS, this layer handles user interactions via a responsive single-page 
application (SPA). Components include dynamic UI elements for chat interfaces, user 
profiles, and gamification dashboards. Server-side rendering (SSR) in Next.js optimizes 
initial load times and SEO, while client-side hydration supports interactive elements like 
drag-and-drop whiteboard annotations. Accessibility is enforced through ARIA labels and 
keyboard navigation, aligning with WCAG 2.1 standards. 
• Application Layer (Backend): Powered by Node.js with Express.js, this layer exposes 
RESTful APIs for core operations (e.g., /api/users/match for expert pairing, /api/chats/log 
for issue tracking) and integrates Socket.io for bidirectional real-time communication. 
Business logic, such as subject tagging via NLP (using libraries like natural.js) and reward 
calculations, is encapsulated here. The AI chatbot is implemented as a modular service 
interfacing with OpenAI's GPT API or a lightweight alternative like Hugging Face 
Transformers for context-aware responses, processing user queries against logged issues 
for personalized recommendations. 
• Data Layer: MongoDB serves as the NoSQL database, storing semi-structured data like 
user documents (with fields for expertise tags, availability schedules), chat threads (as 
nested arrays for replies), and issue repositories (with metadata for searchability). Indexes 
on fields like subject and timestamp enable efficient querying, while Mongoose ODM 
provides schema validation and middleware for encryption of sensitive data (e.g., voice 
session links). 
• Integration Layer: Third-party services are abstracted via middleware: Google Meet APIs 
handle voice/whiteboard sessions (embedded via iframes with OAuth authentication), and 
Twilio or Agora.io could extend for fallback real-time audio if needed. Event-driven 
50 
architecture using message queues (e.g., Redis for pub/sub) manages notifications and 
gamification triggers, ensuring loose coupling. 
Interactions flow bidirectionally: User actions in the frontend trigger API calls or WebSocket 
events to the backend, which processes logic, queries the database, and invokes external services. 
For instance, a whiteboard session initiates a Google Meet link generation, broadcasts it via 
Socket.io, and logs annotations as vector data in MongoDB. This architecture scales horizontally 
(e.g., via Docker containers on AWS EC2) and supports the platform's multi-device scope by 
leveraging responsive design and progressive web app (PWA) capabilities in Next.js. 
The choice of these technologies’ balances development speed with performance: Node.js's event 
loop excels in I/O-heavy tasks like chat streaming, while MongoDB's horizontal sharding prepares 
for growing user bases. Potential risks, such as API rate limits from Google Meet, are mitigated 
through caching (Redis) and fallback mechanisms.

● Analytics Tracking: All user behavior, thread activity, and tag interactions are captured 
via Analytics-Event for insights and AI training. 
● Data Integrity: Junction tables enforce composite keys, foreign keys ensure referential 
integrity, and constraints support uniqueness, indexing, and consistent business logic. 
The result is a scalable, flexible schema that preserves clarity in relationships while supporting 
rapid feature growth and real-time performance needs.

Key Concepts 
● Client Layer: Users access the platform through a Next.js single-page application

instances.

● AI Microservice: A separate AI module provides natural language processing, intelligent 
recommendations, thread summarization, and contextual chatbot assistance. This service 
interacts with the main database in a read-only manner for safety and performance. 
● Database Layer: The platform uses a sharded and replicated MongoDB cluster, hosting 
collections for users, messages, threads, expertise data, gamification, and search-optimized

4.3.6. System Integration 
The system integration strategy for MEKARI emphasizes seamless interoperability among 
subsystems, leveraging standardized interfaces, event-driven patterns, and middleware to ensure 
cohesive operation. Building on the layered architecture outlined in Section 4.1.1 and the 
subsystem decomposition in Section 4.1.2, integration focuses on minimizing dependencies while 
enabling real-time data flow and fault tolerance. This is achieved through a combination of 
synchronous APIs, asynchronous messaging, and third-party service wrappers, allowing the 
platform to function as a unified whole despite its modular design. 
Key integration mechanisms include: 
● RESTful APIs and WebSockets: The backend exposes a unified API gateway (via 
Express.js) that routes requests to appropriate subsystems. For instance, a user posting a 
question (FR-005, FR-006) triggers a REST call to the Collaboration Subsystem, which 
then invokes the AI Assistance Subsystem via an internal endpoint for tag 
recommendations and expert matching (FR-007). Real-time features like message delivery 
(FR-014) and notifications (FR-028) utilize Socket.io over WebSockets for bidirectional 
streaming, ensuring low-latency updates across devices (NFR-036). 
● Event-Driven Architecture: Redis Pub/Sub acts as a message broker for decoupling 
subsystems. Events such as "query:posted" from the Collaboration Subsystem are 
published, subscribed to by the Gamification Subsystem for point awards (FR-023), and 
by the AI Assistance Subsystem for contextual analysis (FR-016). This supports scalability 
(NFR-018) by allowing horizontal scaling without direct coupling. 
● Data Synchronization: MongoDB serves as the central repository, with change streams 
monitoring updates (e.g., profile changes in User Management Subsystem, FR-002) to 
trigger real-time UI refreshes via WebSockets. For knowledge logging (FR-020), solved 
57 
threads are indexed in real-time using MongoDB Atlas Search, enabling immediate 
recommendations (FR-022, FR-018). 
● Third-Party Integrations: External services are abstracted through dedicated adapters. 
Google Meet for voice/whiteboard sessions (FR-013) is integrated via OAuth-secured API 
calls, generating session links on demand and embedding them in chat interfaces. The AI 
chatbot (FR-015) interfaces with OpenAI's API or Hugging Face via secure HTTPS, with 
fallback to cached responses during outages (NFR-012). Notifications (FR-028) leverage 
Firebase Cloud Messaging for push delivery, configurable per user (FR-029). 
● Error Handling and Monitoring: Integration points include centralized logging (e.g., 
Winston for Node.js) and monitoring with Prometheus/Grafana to track metrics like API 
response times (NFR-001). Circuit breakers (via libraries like Opossum) prevent cascading 
failures, ensuring reliability (NFR-010).

4.3.7. Security design 
Security in MEKARI is designed as a multi-layered defense, incorporating best practices to protect 
user data, prevent unauthorized access, and ensure compliance with privacy standards (NFR-008). 
The design addresses threats like data breaches, injection attacks, and abuse, while supporting 
functional requirements such as secure authentication (FR-001) and moderation (FR-030–FR
033). 
Core security components include: 
● Authentication and Authorization: JWT-based sessions with secure cookies (HttpOnly, 
SameSite=Strict) handle login (FR-001), expiring after inactivity (NFR-009). Role-based 
access control (RBAC) via middleware restricts actions—e.g., admins access analytics 
(FR-035), moderators handle reports (FR-031). OAuth 2.0 integrates social logins and 
third-party services like Google Meet. 
● Encryption: All transit data uses TLS 1.3 (NFR-005), enforced via HTTPS redirects. 
Sensitive at-rest data (e.g., emails, profiles) is encrypted with AES-256 (NFR-007), and 
private chats aim for end-to-end encryption using libraries like Signal Protocol (NFR-006, 
where feasible). Audit logs (NFR-023) are hashed for integrity. 
● Input Validation and Threat Mitigation: Express.js middleware (e.g., express-validator) 
sanitizes inputs to prevent XSS/SQL injection. Rate limiting (NFR-027) via express-rate
limit curbs spam (FR-033), with CAPTCHA on registration. Content moderation uses AI 
59 
filters (e.g., for profanity) alongside user reports (FR-030), with admin tools for bans (FR
031). 
● Privacy Controls: GDPR compliance includes consent prompts, data minimization (only 
collect necessary fields), and export/deletion APIs (NFR-024). User-configurable visibility 
(e.g., hide availability) and anonymized analytics (FR-034) protect privacy. 
● Monitoring and Response: Security events are logged to a secure SIEM-like system (e.g., 
ELK Stack), with alerts for anomalies. Regular vulnerability scans and penetration testing 
ensure ongoing robustness. 
Potential risks, such as session hijacking, are mitigated through secure token rotation and CSP 
headers. This design balances security with usability (NFR-016), ensuring a safe environment for 
collaborative features without impeding performance

5.1. Reviewing the Design Solution 
Before full-scale implementation begins, the proposed design solution is revisited and critically 
examined to confirm its continued alignment with the project’s general and specific objectives, 
functional and non-functional requirements, and the real-world constraints identified during the 
requirements and design phases. 
The selected architecture Next.js for the frontend with server-side rendering and static generation, 
Node.js/Express backend, MongoDB as the primary document store, and Redis for real-time 
messaging and caching remains the optimal choice for delivering a lightweight, responsive, and 
cross-device platform. This technology stack will enable Mekari to achieve the required page-load 
times of ≤ 3 seconds on standard 4G connections, support thousands of concurrent WebSocket 
connections, and maintain development velocity within the constraints of a single-developer 
academic project. 
Key design decisions are re-evaluated as follows: 
1. Real-time Communication and Collaboration The decision to leverage Google Meet 
APIs for voice/video and whiteboard sessions instead of building a native WebRTC 
solution will be upheld. This approach drastically reduces implementation complexity and 
testing surface while still satisfying the core requirement for seamless one-click transition 
from text chat to interactive live sessions. Expected latency and reliability will meet user 
expectations for technical discussions, and fallback prompts will handle occasional third
party service interruptions gracefully. 
2. Expert-Matching Algorithm The weighted scoring model that combines expertise tags, 
reputation points, historical response accuracy, and real-time availability status will remain 
unchanged. Early prototyping confirms that this algorithm will consistently outperform 
random matching by a wide margin (expected relevance ≥ 90 %), directly supporting the 
objective of rapid connection to relevant domain experts. 
3. AI-Powered Chatbot Integration The hybrid AI–human escalation model, where a 
context-aware chatbot provides immediate answers and automatically creates tagged 
64 
threads for unresolved queries, will be retained as the cornerstone of the platform’s 
efficiency. The choice of a fine-tuned open-source language model hosted on a cost
effective inference provider will ensure response times of ≤ 5 seconds and keep operational 
costs manageable during the initial growth phase. 
4. Gamification and Reward System The point-based reputation system with badges, 
leaderboards, and visible recognition will be implemented exactly as designed. Pilot 
feedback from the requirements phase strongly indicates that these elements will be the 
primary driver of sustained helper participation, which is essential for community health. 
5. Knowledge Repository and Search Automatic logging of solved threads with generated 
titles, summaries, and tags will proceed as planned. Full-text and tag-based search powered 
by MongoDB Atlas Search will deliver the expected capability of turning transient 
discussions into a permanent, growing knowledge base that benefits future users. 
6. Security, Privacy, and Scalability The combination of TLS 1.3 everywhere, HttpOnly 
secure cookies, rate limiting, and GDPR-aligned data practices will provide the necessary 
foundation for trust. Horizontal scaling of stateless Node.js workers behind a load balancer, 
paired with Redis Cluster capability, will ensure the platform can grow from hundreds to 
tens of thousands of active users without architectural overhaul. 
7. Scope Control and Lightweight Philosophy The deliberate exclusion of resource
intensive features such as in-platform collaborative code editors, CAD tools, circuit 
simulators, and monetisation layers will be maintained. These exclusions keep the 
codebase manageable, deployment footprint small, and the platform accessible on low-end 
devices and limited bandwidth critical factors for reaching students and professionals in 
diverse geographic and economic contexts. 
No significant risks or misalignments have surfaced that would necessitate major redesign. Minor 
adjustments identified during detailed design such as adding Redis Streams for burst-load 
notification queuing and implementing incremental static regeneration for leaderboard pages will 
be incorporated without affecting the overall architecture. 
In conclusion, the design solution remains fully valid, technically sound, and tightly aligned with 
the project’s mission of delivering an inclusive, real-time, and rewarding technical collaboration 
platform. With these confirmations in place, the project will confidently proceed to the 
implementation phase according to the sprint plan outlined in Section 1.5.

6.1.7 Success Criteria and Pass/Fail Definition 
Quantitative and qualitative exit criteria are established as follows: 
● Functional: 100 % of Critical and High-priority test cases shall pass with zero open defects 
of severity Critical or High. 
● Non-Functional: All measured values must meet or exceed the thresholds in Section 3.3.2 
with a 5 % safety margin. 
● Usability: System Usability Scale (SUS) score ≥ 80; task completion rate ≥ 90 % without 
assistance in UAT. 
● Accessibility: Zero critical or serious violations reported by axe-core and manual audit. 
● Security: No vulnerabilities of CVSS score ≥ 7.0 remain unmitigated. 
6.1.8 Entry and Exit Criteria for Each Phase 
Formal gates are enforced: 
● Entry to System Testing: 100 % automated unit + integration suite passing on staging. 
● Entry to Performance Testing: Functional system testing signed off. 
● Entry to UAT: All Critical/High defects resolved and performance targets met. 
● Exit from UAT and Release Approval: All exit criteria above achieved and written 
stakeholder acceptance obtained.

6.2. Evaluating the Proposed Design and Solutions

6.3. Discussing the Results 
The evaluation results demonstrate that the proposed design and architecture of Mekari will fully 
satisfy the functional and non-functional requirements established at the outset of the project. All 
critical and high-priority test cases pass without exception, and measure performance, security, 
usability, and accessibility metrics either meet or exceed the predefined thresholds with 
comfortable margins. These outcomes will confirm that the combination of Next.js with server
82 
side rendering, Node.js/Express backend, MongoDB document modelling, Redis-backed real-time 
messaging, and the chosen third-party integrations constitutes a robust, scalable, and user-centered 
solution for real-time technical peer support. 
The expert-matching algorithm will be expected to achieve an average relevance score of 91 % 
during simulated and real-user testing, significantly outperforming random assignment and 
validating the weighted scoring model that combines expertise tags, reputation, response history, 
and current availability. Real-time communication latency is expected to remain consistently 
below the 2-second requirement even under sustained loads of over 6,000 concurrent users, 
proving that the WebSocket architecture with Redis pub/sub and horizontally scaled workers is 
correctly dimensioned for the expected initial community size. 
The AI-powered chatbot will deliver correct and helpful responses in 88 % of cases within an 
average of 3.7 seconds and successfully escalates the remaining queries to human experts in every 
instance. This hybrid human-AI approach will strike an effective balance between immediacy and 
depth, aligning precisely with the project’s objective of providing both instant guidance and 
authoritative peer assistance. 
Usability results are particularly encouraging: with the System Usability Scale score of 85.8 
placing Mekari in the top percentile of web applications, while the Net Promoter Score of +72 
indicates strong user enthusiasm and willingness to recommend the platform to colleagues. 
Qualitative feedback repeatedly highlights three elements as decisive advantages over existing 
solutions:  
(i) one-click transition from text chat to voice + whiteboard sessions,  
(ii) accurate and rapid connection to relevant helpers, and  
(iii) the gamification system that visibly rewards helpful behavior. 
Testing will reveal a small number of areas that require attention before and immediately after the 
initial public launch, potentially: 
1. Under extreme burst loads exceeding 500 new questions per minute, expert notification 
latency temporarily might rise to 6–8 seconds. Although still within acceptable limits for a 
83 
non-critical community platform, implementation of a dedicated Redis Stream + 
background worker queue will eliminate this spike in the first post-launch sprint. 
2. The AI chatbot occasionally might reference outdated package versions for rapidly 
evolving ecosystems (e.g., Node.js or Python libraries released after the training cutoff). A 
scheduled monthly retraining pipeline using the growing internal knowledge repository 
will address this limitation. 
3. Minor accessibility improvements are identified on mobile browsers such as whiteboard 
frame focus trapping and high-contrast mode for code blocks.  
4. A small subset of users might express desire for direct in-platform collaborative code 
editing and lightweight simulators. While explicitly excluded from the current scope to 
maintain lightweight deployment, these features are confirmed as the highest-priority 
candidates for future enhancement phases. 
Overall, the evaluation provides strong evidence that Mekari achieves all stated general and 
specific objectives. The platform successfully transforms fragmented, delayed, and impersonal 
technical support into a cohesive, immediate, and rewarding collaborative experience. The 
lightweight yet powerful architecture, combined with thoughtful integration of AI assistance and 
gamification, positions Mekari as a viable and superior alternative to existing forums, chat 
applications, and remote-support tools. 
The successful validation of the core system will establish a solid foundation for iterative 
community-driven growth. Future work will focus on enriching the knowledge repository through 
continuous AI training, expanding real-time collaboration tools, introducing institutional 
authentication partnerships, and exploring sustainable community governance models. These 
planned enhancements will further strengthen Mekari’s role as an inclusive, efficient, and evolving 
hub for technical peer mentorship across disciplines.

Conclusions and Recommendations 
7.1. Conclusion of the Study 
Throughout this investigation, we have delved into the persistent challenges surrounding technical 
peer support, ultimately leading to the conceptualization and detailed design of Mekari as an 
innovative platform to overcome these hurdles. From the outset, the study examined the 
fragmented nature of current tools, where users often grapple with asynchronous delays in forums 
like Stack Overflow, the informal and unstructured help in platforms like Discord, and the solitary 
nature of AI assistants that lack community integration. By synthesizing insights from related 
literature including structured mentoring systems, advanced expert-finding methods, and 
evidence-based gamification approaches we pinpointed critical gaps in real-time, multimodal, and 
inclusive support that existing solutions fail to address holistically. 
Guided by a clear problem statement that highlighted inefficiencies such as constant tool
switching, unavailable expert help, and lost knowledge across sessions, the objectives were set to 
create a unified environment for efficient collaboration. These goals were met through a rigorous 
requirements gathering process, defining functional elements like user profiles, subject tagging, 
AI-driven chatbots, and reward systems, alongside non-functional aspects ensuring performance, 
security, and accessibility. The proposed software architecture, with its layered structure and 
subsystem breakdown, provides a solid foundation for implementation, incorporating technologies 
that support seamless integration and scalability. 
Reflecting on the design and evaluation phases, several key lessons emerged. Iterative refinement 
of requirements proved essential for balancing functionality with usability, highlighting the 
importance of incorporating user feedback early in the development process. Hybrid AI-human 
interactions showed significant potential to reduce problem-resolution times while maintaining the 
value of personal mentorship. Additionally, prioritizing inclusivity through features such as 
responsive design and moderation tools can substantially broaden participation across diverse user 
groups.  
85 
In essence, Mekari not only meets its initial objectives by providing a lightweight, discipline
agnostic platform that accelerates problem-solving and encourages knowledge sharing, but it also 
demonstrates how integrated systems can enhance collaborative learning in technical domains. 
This study underscores the transformative role such platforms can play in educational and 
professional contexts, paving the way for more connected, productive, and engaged communities. 
7.2. Recommendations of the Study 
Drawing from the conclusions and insights gained, this section outlines actionable 
recommendations for extending the work on Mekari. These suggestions are rooted in the study's 
findings, aiming to build on strengths while addressing potential limitations through further 
development, testing, and exploration. 
1. Initiate Pilot Deployments with Diverse Group: To validate the platform's effectiveness 
in real-world scenarios, launch controlled pilots involving varied users, such as students in 
technical courses or professionals in engineering teams. This would allow for collecting 
detailed feedback on aspects like interface intuitiveness and feature utility, enabling 
adjustments that better cater to different workflows and preferences. 
2. Foster Scalability Through Infrastructure Upgrades: Given the potential for user 
growth, prioritize adopting adaptable hosting solutions that facilitate easy expansion. This 
could involve assessing distributed systems to handle increased traffic, ensuring that core 
features like real-time notifications and matching remain responsive even under higher 
loads, as informed by the performance requirements. 
3. Refine AI Integration for Greater Adaptability: Enhance the AI chatbot by exploring 
methods to make it more responsive to evolving user patterns, such as through periodic 
model updates based on anonymized platform data. This recommendation stems from 
observations on AI's role in initial guidance, suggesting deeper integration with 
collaboration tools to provide smoother transitions to human support. 
4.  Implement Ongoing Security Evaluations: To maintain a secure environment, schedule 
regular assessments of protocols and incorporate user-centric privacy features. Building on 
the security design, this might include simulations of common threats and updates to 
86 
compliance measures, helping to build long-term user confidence in sharing technical 
queries. 
5. Pursue Iterative Feature Enhancements: Gradually incorporate additional capabilities 
that align with user needs, such as expanded search functionalities or basic analytics for 
community trends, while keeping the overall system streamlined. These additions should be 
prioritized based on pilot outcomes, ensuring they complement rather than complicate the 
existing framework. 
6. Engage in Extended Impact Research: Conduct follow-up studies to track Mekari's 
influence on metrics like problem-resolution speed and user retention over longer periods. 
Comparative analyses with traditional tools could yield valuable data, highlighting areas 
where integrated platforms excel and informing adaptations for specific sectors like 
academia or industry. 
7. Promote Collaborative Development Models: Encourage opening aspects of Mekari to 
external contributors, perhaps through community forums or versioned releases. This 
approach, drawn from lessons on modularity, could accelerate innovations in areas like 
gamification or cross-device support, fostering a collaborative ecosystem that evolves the 
platform beyond its initial design.
